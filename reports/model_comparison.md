# Model comparison - validation split

Made by `python src/train.py`. Training split: 22,685 loans, validation split: 4,862 loans (default rate 21.9%). Cross-validation: 5-fold stratified on the training split. Test split not used.

| Model | Imbalance handling | CV ROC-AUC (mean) | CV std | Train ROC-AUC | Val ROC-AUC | Val PR-AUC | Val KS | Precision @0.5 | Recall @0.5 | F1 @0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| XGBoost | scale_pos_weight = negatives / positives | 0.942 | 0.005 | 0.995 | 0.955 | 0.912 | 0.772 | 0.848 | 0.802 | 0.824 |
| XGBoost + SMOTE | SMOTE oversampling (training rows only) | 0.943 | 0.004 | 0.991 | 0.952 | 0.912 | 0.771 | 0.963 | 0.751 | 0.844 |
| Random Forest | class_weight="balanced_subsample" | 0.930 | 0.006 | 1.000 | 0.940 | 0.896 | 0.748 | 0.983 | 0.712 | 0.826 |
| Logistic Regression | class_weight="balanced" | 0.873 | 0.005 | 0.873 | 0.879 | 0.726 | 0.601 | 0.530 | 0.791 | 0.635 |

Train ROC-AUC is shown only to spot overfitting (a big gap to the validation AUC = the model memorises).
Precision / recall / F1 use threshold 0.5; the real decision cut-offs are chosen in Step 5.

## Compared with the quick baselines (`reports/leakage_check.md`)

- Logistic Regression (untuned, no class weights): 0.878 validation ROC-AUC
- Gradient Boosting (untuned): 0.936 validation ROC-AUC
- Leakage alarm: 0.97. Highest validation ROC-AUC here: 0.955 -> below the alarm.

## Recommendation

Tune **XGBoost** in Step 4: highest validation ROC-AUC (0.955), CV ROC-AUC 0.942 +/- 0.005.

- Tree models beat Logistic Regression by 0.076 validation ROC-AUC (0.955 vs 0.879). They can learn the step patterns from the EDA (grade C -> D, loan_percent_income above 0.30, renters) and their combinations; a straight-line model cannot.
- SMOTE does not clearly help: CV ROC-AUC 0.943 vs 0.942 (difference +0.001, smaller than one CV standard deviation of 0.005) and validation 0.952 vs 0.955. scale_pos_weight is simpler, faster and does not create synthetic loans, so plain XGBoost is preferred.
- Random Forest scores lower (0.940) and memorises the training data (train ROC-AUC 1.000). XGBoost also overfits a little (train 0.995 vs validation 0.955); tuning max_depth, min_child_weight and regularisation in Step 4 should help.
- Validation ROC-AUC is +0.006 to +0.013 away from the CV mean for every model, so this validation split is slightly easier than average. Use the CV mean (not the validation number) as the tuning objective.

## Why is the AUC so high? A perfect rule in the data

The precision-recall chart (14) shows the tree models flag about two thirds of the defaulters with no
false alarm at all. The reason, on the training split:

| Rule | Loans | Defaults | Default rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| loan_percent_income > 0.30 AND renter | 1,650 | 1,650 | 100.0% |
| loan_percent_income > 0.30 AND not renter | 988 | 231 | 23.4% |
| loan_percent_income <= 0.30 AND renter | 9,893 | 2,002 | 20.2% |
| all loans | 22,685 | 4,962 | 21.9% |

Every renter whose loan is more than 30% of income defaulted. This is not leakage (both values are known
at application time), but a rule this clean is very unusual in real lending data and suggests the labels
were at least partly generated by rules. So the AUC on this dataset is likely higher than the same model
would reach on real loans - an honest limitation for the report.
